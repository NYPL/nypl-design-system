## Overview

In all written materials, the Library's voice should support its brand and
reflect its four brand attributes: welcoming, knowledgeable, inspiring, and
dynamic. By speaking in one voice, we provide a consistent experience across
multiple platforms—from printed publications to our website, brochures, flyers,
and signage—while reinforcing the Library's brand.

Of course, there are times when altering tone based on audience is necessary,
such as when talking to kids, teens, or other specialized audiences, or when
using specific platforms, such as social media. But in general, the Library's
voice should remain constant in all communications.

When considering how our brand attributes extend to writing about the Library,
it is helpful to think of the Library's voice as encompassing the "three I's":
`informative`, `inviting`, and `inspiring`. In all written materials, from the
shortest promotional copy to our homepage, e-newsletters, and lengthy
publications, we aim to embody these three principles.

## Informative

In all communications, our primary goal is to provide information about the
Library and what we offer. This means writing should be helpful, instructive,
and knowledgeable, while focusing on the breadth and depth of our offerings and
on our valued staff expertise. Employ simple sentences that convey the Library's
size, impact, and legacy while positioning it as a leader.

## Inviting

Next, all written materials should ensure that readers feel welcomed or invited
to access our offerings. This means the writing should be clear and accessible,
not fussy or stuffy, nor too casual or glib, while placing an emphasis on
inviting readers to make use of our resources. For example, in promotional copy,
we often use active verbs that invite our readers to join us.

## Inspiring

Lastly, in addition to informing readers and inviting them to access our
services, we aim to inspire them to discover new ideas or opportunities at the
Library. In our writing, this means providing specific details meant to engage
users, spark their imaginations, and connect them to new opportunities, or link
the Library to larger ideas or ideals.

### Example

> The New York Public Library offers 93,000 free programs a year across
> its 92 locations, from author talks and performances to exhibitions. Meet your
> favorite author, hear a live concert, or find something new at the Library.

For additional examples of the Library's voice and tone, consult the Library's
website or the latest issue of our e-newsletter NYPL Connect. Creative Services
is also available to help. If you have questions about the Library's voice or
would like additional guidance on your writing, please contact the Creative
Services team within the Communications & Marketing Department by emailing
creativeservices@nypl.org.
