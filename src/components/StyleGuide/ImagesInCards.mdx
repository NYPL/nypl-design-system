## General information

How an image is treated in a UI card should be predicated upon specific use
cases, considering both the intrinsic content of the image and the intended
purpose of the card.

## Aspect ratio

The aspect ratio of an image footprint within a UI card warrants consideration
from both an aesthetic and functional perspective. With that in mind, Product
Managers and Product Designers will make the final decision regarding the
appropriate aspect ratio, while simultaneously accounting for the aspect ratio
of the original image slated for rendering within the UI card.

For example, if the image in a card is used to display a book cover, it may be
best to use a "portrait" aspect ratio like 3:4 or 1:2. Similarly, if the image
in a card is used to display a photograph of a classroom setting, a "landscape"
aspect ratio like 2:1 would be more fitting.

## Cropping

The cropping treatment of an image in a UI card should be determined by the
intent of the image. The determining factor is whether the image is
`illustrative` or `informational`.

### Illustrative

Illustrative images are used to help explain or clarify concepts or ideas. For
example, the images displayed in navigational UI cards that are used heavily
throughout nypl.org would be considered illustrative. The images don't show
specific objects, but instead represent the content or intent of pages like
Staff Picks, Accessibility at NYPL, or Babies &amp; Toddlers reading programs.

An illustrative image rendered in a UI card should be cropped so the image fills
the card's entire image footprint.

### Informational

Informational images are direct representations of real objects—often
photographs, diagrams, or graphs. Examples of informational images are book
covers and digitized materials like prints, photographs, maps, and manuscripts
from NYPL Digital Collections.

When displaying informational images within a card, apply letterboxing to ensure
the entire image is visible. Avoid cropping, as this can obscure critical
content.
